## What can be claimed about contamination

The Chronos-2 paper's real-data table lists weather, power, transport, web, cloud and M4 collections, rather than explicit RUB/CIS exchange rates. M4's finance-related subsets and opaque series identifiers prevent this list from proving universal exclusion. The authors distinguish synthetic-only results from the mixed corpus; their general-benchmark leakage audits do not establish a CBR-specific audit. [Chronos-2 paper, Appendix A and section5.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15821v1).

Synthetic-only pretraining is the strongest available control against memorizing these real FX observations. It still relies on the publisher's stated corpus, and the trained architecture/checkpoint was released after the2023–25 evaluation dates. Every result is therefore a **retrospective capability experiment**, not a demonstration that this model could have been deployed in2023. Selection across already inspected V3/V4 development data is another source of optimism that synthetic pretraining cannot remove.

Searches of primary Hugging Face, arXiv and author repositories did not identify a RUB→AMD/KGS/KZT/TJS/UZS pretrained checkpoint with published weights, a verified training cutoff and an auditable currency corpus. This is a bounded search result, not proof that no private or unindexed model exists. Kronos is the relevant public financial-domain lead; monetary/macroeconomic research papers and language models tagged `rub` are not substitutes for numerical FX forecasting weights.

Kronos is not merely a US-equity model: its appendix lists Moscow Exchange stocks/ETFs, Russian stock indices and1,023 foreign-exchange instruments. It does not list those FX pair identifiers in the cited table, which gives start dates rather than a target-specific cutoff certificate. Hence neither RUB exposure nor exclusion of RUB/CIS evaluation observations can be established from that table alone. [Kronos Appendix, Table13](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02739v1).

Two recent primary financial evaluations reinforce the need for a local benchmark. One studies Treasury changes, EUR/USD volatility and equity spreads and finds transfer gains can coexist with stronger specialized baselines. Another studies five US equities and reports sparse economically reliable improvements over random-walk baselines. Neither establishes RUB/CIS skill. [Marconi2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07296), [Noguer i Alonso and Franklin2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27100).

## Output semantics and implementation audit

Chronos-2 uses time attention plus group attention and directly predicts marginal quantiles. Related currencies may share a group, but windows with different decision dates must not share one: a later window could otherwise reveal an earlier target. Our `cross_learning=False` assigns each five-currency cutoff its own group. [Architecture and inference specification](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15821v1).

The installed Small and Synth checkpoints both expose **13 quantiles**: `.01,.05,.1,.2,.3,.4,.5,.6,.7,.8,.9,.95,.99`. The paper describes a21-quantile head for its reference model. The benchmark records the actual checkpoint configuration rather than silently importing the paper's count. Native public APIs support `fit(..., finetune_mode='full')`, which returns a copy and saves learned weights. [Official implementation](https://github.com/amazon-science/chronos-forecasting/blob/main/src/chronos/chronos2/pipeline.py).

For horizon5, NOW means `min(R[t+1:t+6]) >= R[t]`, not `R[t+5]>=R[t]`. Marginal CDFs imply only the Fréchet bounds `max(0,sum(p_h)-4) <= P(NOW) <= min(p_h)`; multiplying five probabilities silently assumes conditional independence. We supply marginal summaries and bounds to a separately trained event adapter. Consequently **zero-shot refers only to the forecasting backbone**: the NOW probability is supervised and calibrated on historical data. The forecast pinball score remains a separate measurement of the untouched zero-shot backbone.
